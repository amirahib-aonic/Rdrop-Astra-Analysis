The selected protection takes priority over pulse endurance. MF-RG900-0 has 9 A hold and 15.3 A trip specifications at 23 °C. It may interrupt the requested 15 A / 30 second servo pulse. Its trip is thermal and depends on ambient temperature, mounting, load history and airflow. This is the protection option you selected; successful pulse endurance is not guaranteed.
01 / System

The original functions, rebuilt.

Six functional sheets plus a system sheet. On-board inputs are regulated 7.4 V and 5 V only; a 14–18S battery must stay upstream of an appropriately rated external regulator.

Logic power

TLV76750DRVR converts 7.4 V to 5 V for logic. Two SS14 diodes combine that output and external 5 V. TLV1117LV33DCYR supplies 3.3 V with ceramic 0603 input/output decoupling.

The AMS1117 alternative remains an LDO, not a switching step-down regulator. TLV1117LV was selected for ceramic-capacitor stability.

Control & position

ESP32-WROOM-32E-N4, UART programming, boot/reset buttons, voltage sensing and a 20-pin interface. AS5600-ASOM operates at 3.3 V with 4.7 kΩ I²C pull-ups; its magnetic center is retained.

Dual optical gates

Two TSAL6200 emitters with individual 2N7002P drivers. TEFT4300 supplies an analog beam signal; TSSP4038 supplies a 38 kHz demodulated digital signal. Original footprint origins and optical pad centers are preserved.

02 / Power engineering

Servo current bypasses the logic regulators.

External regulated source→F101 · 7.4 V→J106 · servo

Fit F101 and leave F102 empty. The servo receives external 7.4 V.

Copper & transitions

The incoming servo bus is 4.5 mm wide. The fused output uses both layers; its final connector approach uses 2 mm traces on each face. The return has 3 mm traces on each face and ground pours. The external 5 V input changes layers through a 12-via array inside TP103; both ground wire pads also have 12-via arrays.

For 70 µm copper, a 4.5 mm × 70 mm bus has approximately 3.8 mΩ resistance at 20 °C before solder joints and component resistance. At 15 A this segment dissipates approximately 0.86 W. This is a resistance estimate, not a validated temperature-rise result.

J106 is Phoenix Contact 1711738, nominal 24 A. Use appropriate wire gauge, screw torque and strain relief. The small 2.54 mm monitoring/programming headers are not servo power connectors.

Thermal boundary

At 7 A the fuse's specified initial maximum 9.2 mΩ resistance produces about 0.45 W; at 15 A it produces about 2.07 W before its resistance rises. Post-trip resistance can be higher.

MF-RG900 hold current derates to 7.2 A at 50 °C and 6.4 A at 60 °C. A 7 A load therefore has little margin in a hot enclosure. Check the fuse at actual ambient and duty cycle.

At 8.4 V input and 0.5 A logic load, the 7.4 V→5 V LDO dissipates 1.7 W. The 3.3 V LDO dissipates roughly 0.6–0.7 W after the diode. Ground thermal vias and a 22 × 18 mm two-face 3.3 V cooling area are provided. Sustained full-load operation needs temperature measurement; design around ≤250 mA average logic current until tested.

External regulator selection matters. The named UBEC-10A-HV(3–14S)-V1 is rated for up to 14S, so it must not be connected to an 18S pack. Hobbywing's 25 A HV family supports 3–18S and 7.4 V output; confirm the exact purchased model and pulse rating. An unspecified APM 5 V output must be treated as a logic supply until its 7 A continuous / 15 A for 30 seconds capability is established.
Assembly and input limits

Never fit both F101 and F102: that joins the external 5 V and 7.4 V sources. The on-board 5 V LDO is for logic only and does not supply servo current. TP101/TP102 accept the regulated 7.4 V pair; TP103/TP104 accept the external 5 V pair. Retained conductive standoffs are mechanical/regulator interfaces; their manufacturer does not specify a 15 A electrical rating, so use the dedicated wire pads for servo supply current. Connect no raw battery voltage, and verify polarity before energizing.

J105 = GND / 7.4 V monitoring / unused. J201 and J401 expose low-current 3.3 V monitoring only; budget external loads separately. No reverse-polarity, overvoltage, or flyback-energy qualification has been completed. Measure servo rail transients and use external clamping or regulator output capacitance if its specification requires it.

Firmware and commissioning requirements

Drive both IR emitters with independent 38 kHz modulation. The 220 Ω emitter resistors set approximately 9 mA peak current at 3.3 V; verify optical margin under sunlight and motor interference. TEFT4300's 10 kΩ / 100 nF collector circuit averages the modulation with a 1 ms time constant, so use a calibrated analog threshold. GPIO34/35/36 are input-only. Read AS5600 at I²C address 0x36 and verify magnet status and the angle direction mechanically.

The analog voltage divider reports approximately 2.08 V at an 8.4 V input. Calibrate ADC attenuation and reference; do not apply raw pack voltage. CAN is non-isolated and requires a shared signal ground. R401 is DNP unless this node terminates the bus. GPIO5 is a strap pin: avoid external drive that changes its boot state. Boot uses GPIO0; hold BOOT while resetting for download.

Set servo and IR outputs low at startup. A hardware servo PWM pull-down is provided; firmware must require an explicit valid armed state before motion, debounce sensors, detect timeouts and fail safely on reset or communication loss. Firmware is not included or validated in this hardware package.

Prototype test plan — required before operation
  1. Unpowered inspection: verify one fitted fuse, connector polarity, isolation between 5 V and 7.4 V, solder joints, and optical lead orientation.
  2. Power with a current-limited bench source, first on 5 V and then 7.4 V. Check 3.3 V, reset, programming and ADC scaling without a servo.
  3. Measure supply stability during ESP32 radio traffic and servo motion. Scope 3.3 V and servo input during turn-on, braking and current steps; verify the external regulator remains in specification.
  4. Apply 5 A, 7 A, then the specified 15 A / 30 second load with an electronic load or representative servo. Record fuse trip time, voltage drop, connector and copper temperatures. A trip during the pulse may be consistent with the chosen fuse target.
  5. Repeat in the actual enclosure at the highest ambient and intended airflow. Verify LDO junction margin using package temperature and manufacturer thermal data; check protection recovery after a trip.
  6. Test magnetic sensing across full rotation and verify beam detection with ambient light, vibration, motor noise and real bait. Check reset, lost-command and blocked-mechanism behavior with an unloaded attachment before flight.
